Poems

Ten poems on place, violence, faith, politics, memory, and the cultural life of home.

Writing by Benjamin Armstrong Zugu

01

DAN-ANACHA I

You were once prosperous
But now sapped of glory
Your fortune drowned in innocent blood
And your peace, torn to shards.
Your holy monastery vilely desecrated
As general terror reign supreme.
Abandonment became the only reprieve
From this unprecedented quagmire.
Your movement at a snail pace
And walked so slowly
To catch the flying world.

One day, I shall return to you
After many years of wandering
And I will make my offering
At the feet of my lovely abode.
I will rebuild you, the holy places
They raped and plundered
And make you fine again
With black wood and bronze.

02

DAN-ANACHA II

Dan-anacha my land, fecund
Progenitor of plenty
Of mammals and grains.

Fortune and fame, your gain
Your efforts richly crowned
With greatness and growth.

But repeated onslaught
By marauding herders
Has made you frail.

You were a bubbling city
With the speed of a cheetah
Now a lame duck.

You are now a byword
A mere footstool for giants
Who laugh at you.

I raise my palms skyward
In supplication to the Almighty
For peace and restoration.

03

MAIDUGURI

Once vibrant with splendor
Eminence crowned your labour
Home of peace to all
The pauper as well as the wealthy
Excelling in life's endeavors
Your fame rose high
And reverberated in the horizon
Serene like the crystal crescent.

Now you wallow in squalor;
Streets stripped of splendor
Left littered with carcasses,
Of your butchered children.
Your inhabitants scattered in quandary
Struggling with hunger, shelterless
The mean harmattan humming
On their naked bodies.
Gbum! Gbum!! Gbum!!!
Another explosion on their supple bodies.

04

FOREWARNED

Forewarned but not heeded
Of the nation's degeneration to chaos
A patriot was reprimanded
For his timely foresight:
“Nigeria jaga, jaga.”

Time brought its confirmation
Political gladiators' activities
Reduced democracy to democrazy
As mayhem and onslaught is let loose:
“Everything scata, scata.”

Sadly, their heinous acts unchecked
In fact, aided by the masses
Who themselves victims and victors
Reducing the nation to nothingness:
“Poor man dey sofa, sofa.”

Now, another chapter awaits
For crooked paths to be straightened
Or be forever doomed
For whatever choices made
“Gbosa, gbosa, gunshot ina de air.”

05

ELECTION

“Na how much u go pay?
Na how much u wan sell?
Oga, bring five hundred naira
Abeg, collect two hundred.”
So they bargain away
Their inner conscience
For crispy mint and rhetoric.

Further, actions matched with disdain
Stupidity and ignorance:
While the unworthy candidates
Sleep soundly themselves,
In secure, magnificent edifices;
Ignorant, gullible electorates
In insecure, horrible slums
Unleash mayhem upon selves
For allegiance and affiliations.

Alas! Great is human harvest
Like ripe grains in the filled.
Again, the trumpet sounds
Will you sell your conscience?
Or make good, your essence?

06

HYPOCRISY?

Chaste!
Dawn, grey and misty
Byways, opaque, translucent
Spritely donned garbs
Precede the holy hour.

The priest on the altar sermons
Believers throng to the see
The holy book gives instruction.
In frenzy, monasteries shaken;
Amen! Amen!! Applaud all.

The muezzini atop a minaret
Summons adherents to prayer
Peace! The imam proclaims
In unison and sobriety
Al-fatihah reigns supreme.

Why then the vices and carnage?

07

SAINTS

They are donned in purple apparel
With adorned spritely diadems
Encompassed by affluence and fame
Reverberating on celestial frame
And echoing same old tunes.
They reflect goodness but do none;
All wolves covered in sheep's skin.
They possess sharpened lips
For several aborted vows.
With hands crimsoned in innocent blood
And wardrobes filled with skeletons.
They sleep in comfortable beddings
While the masses holed up in slums
Sleep on cardboard beds and stinks.
They are saviours on the outer layer
Usurpers in internal frames
Beguiled by oratory and treachery
But naught to naught attain
Their type abound in my area.

08

THE REVEREND POLITICIAN

It is thanksgiving service
The politician occupies
A special seat on the podium
His brocade shines to blinding point
When he rises to speak.

The sermon turns to a campaign
And the reverend priest intones
“No voter’s card, no Eucharist.”
He praises the generosity
Of the amiable politician
“You shall succeed, where others fail.”

Elsewhere, the imam led the sallah
In a multimillion naira mosque
Constructed by an assistant director
In the ministry of public affairs
And the imam praises his generosity
Amidst the people living in squalor
And the alhaji brandishes his gold teeth
“All to the glory of Allah.”

At the rally; a holy assembly
“One God, Allah, One God, Allah”
And the crowd is in frenzy.
Afterwards, “we are Christians”
“And we are Muslims”

As many fall in clashes
The reverend politician laughs
At their stupidity and ignorance.

09

GREY (To my white hairs)

My head, a garland of black hairs
Which shone and shimmered
In the rays of the sun
And on moonlight nights
When the cool breeze ruffled them.

Then suddenly, white hairs spotted
Minor invaders to the abundance.
At first, received them with indifference
Thus, dyed to turn them sooth
But hair turned tan, than black.

Finally, accepted them wholly
As mark of ingrained sagehood
Then, allowed them to grow peacefully
In blend of black and white
Thus, white hairs now adorn my head.

10

KPATU (Tiv dance)

His wriggles assented to the rhythm
And his patterned body shimmers
Attuned to the tremolos beats
Produced from natural hide drums
By zestful drummers.

His muscles rippled under his skin
Revealing practiced steps and styles
With a cat skin he danced
The symbol of his art
Emulating deep ancestral core.

The crowd in frenzy, gesticulated
The arena so gaily and agog
Crescendoing with deafening applause
Displaying shimmering soot bodies
In the moon's incandescence.

I watched with much relish
Rich communal ties portrayed
What to me, civilization robbed.
